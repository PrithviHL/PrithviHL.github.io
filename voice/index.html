How it works

  2. Speech detectionwaits for a word, then checks 9 shifted 1 s windows
  3. MFCC features13 coefficients × 66 frames
  4. int8 neural networkEdge Impulse EON-compiled
  5. Commandshown on the "OLED", drives the robot

This is the exact C++ from the ESP32 build (MFCC preprocessing, TensorFlow Lite Micro kernels and the trained int8 model), compiled with Emscripten. It was trained in Edge Impulse on Google's Speech Commands dataset (thousands of real speakers) plus synthetic voices for “turn on”, “turn off”, “open” and “close”, with extra noise and unknown classes. On speakers it never heard it is 74% accurate across all 28 classes, and the page looks at each word through 9 slightly shifted windows and averages them. It acts when the average is confident or the top command clearly beats the runner-up (then it is right about 87% of the time); otherwise the screen shows its best guess with a question mark. On the ESP32 it needs 12 ms, 22 KB of RAM and 72 KB of flash. Speak one command at a time; “turn on” works better than “on” alone.
